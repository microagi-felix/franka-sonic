# REPORT — FR3 handover bake-off, round 2 (P7–P10)

**What this is.** Two ways of driving the same pair of Franka FR3 arms through
the same block-handover task, trained on the same data with the same recipe and
measured on the same rollouts:

- **Lane A** — GR00T N1.7 3B fine-tuned to emit **absolute joint targets**
  directly (16-D: 7 + 7 joints, 2 grippers).
- **Lane B** — the same GR00T fine-tuned to emit a **64-D SONIC motion token**
  per step, decoded to joint targets by a SONIC RL decoder (plus the same two
  gripper columns, which bypass the decoder).

Round 1 (P0–P6) was a pipeline proof on 76 scripted demos and a 2000-step
fine-tune; it measured lane A 0/20 and lane B 1/20 and could not compare
anything. Round 2 (P7–P10) gave **both lanes the same much larger training set
(891 episodes / 722 576 frames), the same 20 000-step fine-tune, the same
checkpoint-selection rule, and 200 rollouts per row**. Nothing differs between
the lanes except the action interface. This report is round 2; section 10 keeps
round 1 for the record.

Generated by `python3 harness/report/aggregate.py` on {{GENERATED}} from the run
folders in appendix B. Every count, interval and verdict sentence below is
computed from those folders' `eval_results.csv` and episode JSONs — the prose
lives in `harness/report/REPORT_template.md` and holds no numbers of its own, so
re-running the script cannot leave a stale claim behind.

GR00T N1.7 samples its actions, so a seed pins the spawn and not the rollout.

- {{B_VERDICT}}

{{CEILING_TABLE}}

**The two ceilings are not the same height, and that asymmetry is structural,
not a flaw in the experiment.** Lane A's interface is the labels themselves, so
its oracle is near-perfect by construction. Lane B's interface is a learned
decoder, so its oracle carries the decoder's own error. A reader comparing the
lanes has to decide which question is being asked: "which stack completes more
handovers" (the absolute column) or "which VLA learned more of what its
interface allows" (the ÷ ceiling column). This report gives both and asserts
neither as *the* answer.

Where the B-oracle loses its episodes is the useful part: **25 of its failures
are at the first grasp, 29 at the second, 1 at milestone 0** — decoder
arm-position error at the grasp instant, not a gripper or timing problem (the
grippers bypass the decoder entirely). P5 measured the tolerance directly: the
A-oracle with a constant offset added to the left joint-1 target scores 20/20 at
+0.02 rad (≈ 1.2 cm at the hand), 17/20 at +0.05 rad (≈ 3 cm) and 0/20 at
+0.10 rad (≈ 6 cm). The right arm's blind re-grasp of the cube the left arm
placed is the 1–3 cm constraint, against the decoder's ≈ 1.0–1.4 cm grasp-frame
flange error — a margin that is thin by construction.

## 9. What these numbers do not support

Read this section before quoting anything above.

1. **20-rollout screens select; they do not measure.** No number in section 1 or
   2 comes from a screen. Round 2 walked back at least four readings that had
   been taken from 20-rollout evidence: lane A's `checkpoint-20000` screen was
   first recorded as 1/7 (a watcher resolved "the run I just made" by mtime and
   picked a 200-rollout row that was 7 episodes in — see item 6); P8's ONNX
   export selection had to be redone over 60 episodes after the 20-rollout
   ranking proved unstable; P5's decoder ranking moved by 3–4 successes between
   repeats of the identical pair; and lane B's 13/20 at 17500 against 7/20 at
   20000 was read overnight as a decline. **Section 2's spread rows are the
   measured version of that same warning.**
2. **Mean progress is not a selection criterion.** Lane A's `checkpoint-5000`
   has the highest mean progress of its entire lane (0.467) and zero successes:
   it reaches, lifts and places at centre in 90 % of episodes and then stalls.
   Ranking on mean progress picks it. The pre-registered rule ranks on
   successes first for exactly this reason, and section 2 leads with the
   milestone vector rather than the mean.
3. **One training seed per lane.** Each lane is one fine-tune run. The
   confidence intervals above are over *evaluation* sampling only; they say
   nothing about how much of the gap between the lanes would survive a second
   training seed. Given that adjacent checkpoints of one run differ by up to
   9 successes in 20 (section 4), seed-to-seed variance is plausibly of the same
   order as any lane difference reported here. **This is the single largest
   limitation of the comparison.**
4. **The evaluation box is ≈ 1.5 % of the training spawn volume.** Training
   spawns were drawn from ±0.09 m in x/y and ±0.75 rad in yaw; the evaluation
   draws from ±0.015 m and ±0.40 rad — about 13 of the 891 training episodes
   land inside the box the policies are graded in. Both lanes are graded on a
   near-centre slice of what they were trained on, so these rates are an
   optimistic reading of either stack's behaviour over the training
   distribution, and say nothing about behaviour outside it.
5. **Each lane trained across two run folders**, with a mid-run storage switch
   and a `--resume-from-checkpoint` resume (section 7). The resume is verified
   by the continuity of the loss curve and the checkpoint interval, not by a
   bit-for-bit argument.
6. **A harness bug touched this phase's own bookkeeping.** The P9 screening
   watcher resolved a finished screening to the newest eval folder by mtime,
   which is wrong as soon as two runs of the same lane and stage overlap — which
   P10 does by design, running 200-rollout rows into run roots the screens were
   still using. It mis-filed lane A's `checkpoint-20000` screen as 1/7 instead of
   16/20. It was caught by reading `eval_results.csv` of the named folder
   directly, and the same root cause had already bitten the P6 aggregate. The
   aggregator behind this report now identifies every run by what
   `harness/bakeoff.py` stamped into its own `config.json` and takes explicit
   `--row <label>=<folder>` paths; nothing here is resolved by recency.
7. **The ONNX export path is not deterministic.** Two exports of the same SONIC
   checkpoint were found to differ in 10 of 30 initializers — exactly the g1
   encoder's ten, by up to ~15 % relative — after which the two encoders agreed
   on 0.0 % of token rows on a 300-frame clip. The fault is intermittent per
   export run, not per checkpoint: two other exports of a different checkpoint
   were byte-identical. Every export used in round 2 was verified before use
   (`[check] VERDICT: OK` on the labelling round-trip), and lane B's rows here
   run the verified pair. But it means an unverified export is not evidence
   about a decoder, and any lane-B number is conditional on the export that
   produced it.
8. **One task, one architecture pair, one demo style.** The demos are scripted
   MimicGen episodes from 30 sources, so both policies inherit a single motion
   style and neither has seen human variation. One task instance (dual-FR3 cube
   handover), one VLA (GR00T N1.7 3B), one decoder family (SONIC). Nothing here
   generalises to other tasks, other VLAs or other token spaces.
9. **The oracles bound each lane's interface, not its policy** (section 6), and
   they are measured on the recorded demo spawns rather than the seeded
   evaluation spawns — so they are ceilings for a slightly different, and
   easier, distribution than the policy rows are graded on.

## 10. Round 1 (P0–P6) — kept for the record

Round 1 was the pipeline proof: 76 scripted-demo episodes, a 2000-step
fine-tune, 20 evaluation rollouts, lane A 0/20 and lane B 1/20 against an
A-oracle of 20/20 and a B-oracle of 19/20. Its conclusion was that both lanes
were starved, which is what round 2 tested. The deviations it recorded, all of
which still constrain round 2:

**Pod and infrastructure**

- No CUDA toolkit on the pod: DeepSpeed needs a `CUDA_HOME` stub with a fake
  `nvcc` (`harness/env/cuda_home_stub`); nothing is compiled. NCCL's P2P and
  shared-memory transports fault between devices, so every fine-tune ran over
  loopback sockets (`NCCL_P2P_DISABLE=1 NCCL_SHM_DISABLE=1`). A 4-rank launch
  faults outright; both round-2 fine-tunes therefore ran on 2 ranks.
- Kit's portable-mode dirs under `/isaac-sim/kit` must be chowned (the
  authorised AGENTS.md rule-j exception) or every Isaac env creation dies.
  `nvidia-smi` is absent; the allocator uses its own probe. Devices count as
  usable below 40 GiB used, the foreign memory on most of them being idle.
- Storage: Lustre home is 99 % full on a shared 70 TB filesystem and drains from
  outside this pod — 1200 → 717 GB in 3.5 h on 2026-09-03, and ≈ 1.6 TB
  overnight during round 2. The floor is 600 GB free, below which run folders go
  to instance-local `/tmp/franka-sonic`, which does not survive a pod restart.
  Each full GR00T checkpoint is 34 GB (27 GB of it DeepSpeed optimizer state).
- The allocator's job name is derived from the run-folder name, which is unique
  only per run root; a collision released a live claim once and double-booked a
  device.

**Demo set**

- No human teleop: scripted MimicGen sources recorded at 50 Hz. MimicGen's
  keep-rate is not captured (Isaac Lab prints its tally to a block-buffered
  stdout that Kit's hard exit never flushes).
- On the 0.20 m angled rig the start slot sits exactly at the centre pad's
  0.10 m zone radius, so MimicGen saw "placed at centre" true at frame 0 and
  refused the subtask boundaries; generation runs from a copy with that signal
  zeroed before the first grasp (`harness/data/fix_subtask_signals.py`).
- Lane A's arm label is `ik_target_clamped` (the recorder's differential-IK
  joint command clipped to the FR3 joint limits), chosen by measurement against
  raw IK, per-step-clipped and next-state alternatives: it is the only one that
  replays the demos. It carries saturated first-chunk commands (2–4 rad swings
  at the arm's first move) that a policy copies, which was round 1's diagnosed
  lane-A failure mode.
- GR00T stacks all camera views into one tensor after its shortest-edge resize,
  so the 424×240 wrist videos are rescaled to the top camera's 640×360 (one
  resolution for all views); the live servers rescale their frames the same way.
- Dataset column order `[Lq7 | Rq7 | Lg Rg]` versus the sim's wire order
  `[Lq7, Lg, Rq7, Rg]`: converted inside both policy servers.

**Lane B embodiment, controller and decoder**

- No SMPL-X body models on the pod, so the motion library is demo-only: the
  token space covers tabletop handover reaching and nothing else. GMR is
  registered for the dual FR3 as a dormant path.
- Isaac Sim 5.1's MJCF importer is not usable headless; the Isaac side spawns a
  URDF generated from the same MJCF. The URDF importer turns joint `friction`
  into a PhysX friction coefficient, so URDF dynamics are 0/0. Self-collision is
  off; inter-arm contact is not modelled.
- Joint-6 convention: SONIC's q6 = FR3 q6 − 2.5307 rad, because the demos drive
  the left joint 6 beyond π and the motion library's axis-angle round trip wraps
  by 2π. Action scale 0.25 rad per unit; PD gains 400/80.
- **The two decoder failures that cost the most, both cross-environment
  mismatches rather than tracking failures.** *Plant mismatch*: SONIC trained
  with gravity ON while the handover env spawns both arms with
  `disable_gravity=True` and different PD gains, so the decoders had learned to
  command above the reference to cancel sag and put the left hand 5–8 cm too
  high — accurate and wrong. *Reset mismatch*: SONIC resets ON the reference
  WITH the reference joint velocity while the handover env starts the arm at
  rest, so the first ≈ 25 frames of every episode ran 9–11 cm off the demo. The
  fixes (train on the deployment plant; add reset position/velocity noise) are
  the recipe both round-2 decoders inherit.
- **The teacher-forced replay error cannot rank decoders.** It stayed inside
  0.041–0.051 rad across a series whose 20-rollout ceiling moved from 1/20 to
  19/20. Only the oracle ranks a decoder — which is why gate P5's rule (a
  B-oracle at ≥ 15/20 before any lane-B fine-tune) exists.
- `simulation_app.close()` never returns headless on this pod; probes and
  stages hard-exit, and the bakeoff wall-clock cap kills by recorded PID.

## 11. What breaks at scale — deltas for a real run

1. **Training budget is the first thing to fix, not the last.** Round 2's own
   series (section 4) shows both lanes acquiring the task in their final
   quarter with the loss still falling. 20 000 steps compared two under-trained
   policies; a real run should train until the *screening curve* plateaus, not
   until a step budget expires, and should screen at ≥ 100 rollouts so the
   plateau is visible above sampling noise.
2. **Demos.** 891 scripted-source MimicGen episodes from 30 scripted sources
   still give one motion style. A real run needs human/teleop sources, the
   MimicGen keep-rate captured, and a validation split held out of both the
   fine-tune and lane B's motion library.
3. **Evaluate on the training distribution, or train on the evaluation one.**
   The ≈ 1.5 % overlap between the evaluation box and the training spawn volume
   (section 9 item 4) makes both rates hard to interpret. Either widen the
   evaluation box to the training box and accept lower rates, or match the two —
   round 3 does the latter and must say so in its own report.
4. **Seeds.** At least three training seeds per lane. Everything in section 9
   item 3 follows from having one.
5. **Lane A's labels.** `ik_target_clamped` replays but carries saturated
   first-chunk commands the policy copies. Either record demos with a
   bounded-excursion controller, retrain on next-state labels with a slower
   oracle expectation, or add an inference-side excursion clamp and measure it.
6. **Lane B's motion library and decoder.** The full retargeted upper-body
   subset instead of the demos' own trajectories; SONIC RL at 4096 envs × 8 GPUs
   for ≥ 24 h instead of ~1.5 h warm-started off a chain of short runs; train on
   the deployment plant from iteration 0; self-collision on. And a deterministic
   export path — the non-determinism in section 9 item 7 is a correctness bug,
   not a nuisance.
7. **Instrumentation.** The commanded 16-D target logged per 50 Hz step in the
   run folder, so jerk and excursion can be reported at all; an open-loop eval
   for lane B as well as lane A; and a run-resolution convention that never uses
   mtime (section 9 item 6).
8. **Real cell.** Both lanes emit absolute joint targets at 50 Hz. Running
   either on the physical FR3s needs a joint-impedance controller on the robots,
   which the repo does not have today, plus the per-step excursion cap from
   `policy_convention.yaml` (0.60 rad) enforced on the robot side. Lane B
   additionally needs the decoder's 10-step proprio history reproduced exactly
   on hardware, and a plant whose gravity handling and gains match what the
   decoder was trained on. Neither stack is closer to hardware than the other.
