- **95 % CI**: exact (Clopper–Pearson) binomial interval on the success count.
- **milestone reach rates**: `handover_rubric()` in the franka repo, six
  milestones ordered by dependency (left reaches → left lifts → placed at
  centre → right reaches → right lifts → placed at end); milestone k counts
  as reached when `criteria_reached ≥ k` in `episode_<k>_result.json`.
- **mean progress**: the evaluator's own `progress` = milestones reached / 6,
  averaged over episodes.
- **median steps-to-success**: episode length of the successful episodes

  placement was confirmed); `n/a` when no episode succeeded.

  difference over it. Nothing in the run folders lets it be reconstructed, so
  it is left blank rather than estimated.

## 5. Oracles — what the two calibration rows say

- **A-oracle 20/20** is the sanity floor and it holds: the export
  episodes' `ik_target_clamped` joint labels and binary grip labels, replayed
  on the recorded cube spawns through the same evaluator, reproduce the
  handover every time. The dataset, the joint-position env, the wire
  contract and the rubric are sound, so lane A's policy number is meaningful.
- **B-oracle 19/20** is lane B's ceiling: the same episodes' encoder
  tokens, streamed through the decoder at 50 Hz with no VLA in the loop.
  It is open: 19/20 (95 %, mean progress 0.958), at or above the 15/20 threshold gate P5 requires and 5 points from the A-oracle's 20/20. The decoder that lane B's policy server runs executes the labels its own encoder wrote. The decoder behind this row is P5's winner, `jp20` last.pt
  (iteration 2848), trained on lane A's plant — gravity off, PD 400/80,
  `harness/lane_b/robots_dual_fr3_lanea.py`. Its teacher-forced replay is
  0.045 rad on the wire target / 0.019 rad measured, body 0.005 m, grasp-frame
  flange error L 1.0 / R 1.3 cm, and it scored 19/20 in P5's ceiling test,
  reproduced episode for episode by an independent end-to-end re-run
  (`oracle_b-14` at 22:43 UTC, `oracle_b-17` at 22:56 UTC, the same single
  failure both times); P6 re-ran that same ONNX/token pair once more so that
  the row above would be this decoder's and not the runner-up's (section 6).
  The single failure is the residual mode: the handover env starts the arm
  at rest while SONIC resets it on the reference with the reference velocity,
  and episode 15's left grasp breaks on the resulting start transient. P2/P3's
  decoder (`jp2`, replay 0.66–0.77 rad, B-oracle 0/20) is history, and so is
  every other gravity-on decoder P5 tried (`jp7`, `jp11`, `jp13`, `jp14`,
  `jp15`, `jp18` — all 0/20); see section 6 for why.
- **Lane B's 1/20 is therefore readable as a VLA number.** With the ceiling at 19/20, lane B's row measures GR00T over the token space driving a decoder that can execute those tokens — the comparison the bake-off asked for. It remains one checkpoint of one 2000-step fine-tune over 20 rollouts, with the interval quoted in section 1, and it is bounded above by the ceiling, not by the A-oracle.
- **Lane A's 0/20 against an A-oracle of 20/20** is a policy/label problem,
  diagnosed in P1: open loop the policy tracks the demos to 0.06 rad MAE
  except in the first chunk, where it reproduces the labels' saturated
  differential-IK commands (2–4 rad swings on joints 1/3/6/7 at the arm's
  first move); closed loop those targets throw the arm into a pose it never
  recovers from. The label was chosen because it is the only one that
  replays the demos (next-state and delta-clamped labels failed the oracle).
  The two candidate fixes (an inference-side per-replan excursion clamp, or
  retraining on next-state labels with a slower oracle expectation) were
  recorded and not run.

## 6. Deviations and decisions taken in P0–P6

Every item is in `plan/STATUS.md` with its timestamp; this is the list a
reader needs to weigh the table.

**Pod and infrastructure**

- Only 2 of the 8 GPUs were allocatable during P0–P3 (devices 5 and 7; the
  others hold foreign memory). PLAN.md's parallel schedule (A fine-tune 2 ‖
  SONIC RL 4 ‖ eval 1–2) became sequential: SONIC RL on one GPU with 2048 envs,
  fine-tunes on 2 GPUs, evals with the policy server and Isaac on the same
  device (replan latency ≈ 85–90 ms against a 400 ms replan period). From
  16:10 UTC all 8 devices were allocatable after the allocator's idle-memory
  threshold was raised from 1 GiB to 40 GiB (`fc7d22b`, Felix's call: the
  foreign allocations on 0–4 and 6 are idle); P5 then ran up to seven trainers
  plus replay and oracle jobs concurrently.
- No CUDA toolkit on the pod: DeepSpeed needs a `CUDA_HOME` stub with a fake
  `nvcc` (`harness/env/cuda_home_stub`); nothing is compiled. NCCL's P2P and
  shared-memory transports fault between the two devices, so both fine-tunes
  ran over loopback sockets (`NCCL_P2P_DISABLE=1 NCCL_SHM_DISABLE=1`);
  measured step rate 0.76–0.77 steps/s, not the bottleneck.
- Kit's portable-mode dirs under `/isaac-sim/kit` had to be chowned (the
  authorised rule-j exception) before any Isaac env would render.
  `nvidia-smi` is absent; the allocator uses its own probe.
- Storage: Lustre home was 99 % full through P0–P3 and drained ≈ 100 GB/h from
  other users. The floor was raised to 600 GB free at 03:33 UTC; from 07:20 UTC
  every new run folder (all of P3: token labels, lane B dataset, lane B
  fine-tune, lane B eval, B-oracle) landed on instance-local
  `/tmp/franka-sonic`, which does not survive a pod restart. Those P3 artifacts
  are still on `/tmp`; the small ones were copied to
  `~/runs/franka-sonic/lane_b/final/` and the lane B checkpoint-2000 model
  files to `…/final/2026-09-03_finetune/` (6.5 GB). By 17:32 UTC free space had
  recovered and `harness/bakeoff.py` routed runs back to home, so every P5 and
  P6 run folder — SONIC RL, exports, replays, token labels, the lane B
  fine-tune, its eval and the B-oracles — is on Lustre home, which had ≈ 2.6 TB
  free when P6 started. Each GR00T checkpoint is 34 GB (27 GB of it DeepSpeed
  optimizer state), so the prescribed `--save-steps 500` wrote 136 GB per lane.

**Demo set (P0/P1)**

- No human teleop: 10 scripted MimicGen sources recorded at 50 Hz (the
  recorder's goto/grip/settle step budgets had to be scaled with the rate),
  80 generated episodes, of which 76 replay to success at 50 Hz with cameras;
  the 4 that do not are flagged and excluded everywhere downstream.
- MimicGen's keep-rate was not recorded: Isaac Lab prints its tally to a
  block-buffered stdout that Kit's hard exit never flushes. From the per-episode
  generation time it was roughly one accepted episode per 2–3 trials; the
  ≥ 50 % target is unverified.
- On the 0.20 m angled rig the start slot sits exactly at the centre pad's
  0.10 m zone radius, so MimicGen saw the "placed at centre" signal true at
  frame 0 and refused the subtask boundaries. A copy of the annotated file with that
  signal zeroed before the first grasp (`harness/data/fix_subtask_signals.py`)
  was generated from instead; no upstream edit.
- Lane A's arm label is `ik_target_clamped` (the recorder's differential-IK
  joint command clipped to the FR3 joint limits), measured on one episode
  against the alternatives: raw IK 6/6 milestones but commands up to ≈ 10 rad
  outside the limits, clipped 6/6 with a sane range, per-step move clipped to
  0.3 rad 4/6, next measured joint position 4/6. The A-oracle and the dataset
  converter share the label function.
- Dataset route HDF5 → GR00T v2 directly (`lerobot` is not on the pod, so
  NVIDIA's v3 → v2 script is unusable). GR00T stacks all camera views into
  one tensor after its shortest-edge resize, so the 424×240 wrist videos were
  rescaled to the top camera's 640×360 (one resolution for all views); the
  live servers rescale their frames the same way.
- Dataset column order `[Lq7 | Rq7 | Lg Rg]` versus the sim's wire order
  `[Lq7, Lg, Rq7, Rg]`: converted inside both policy servers.

**Lane B embodiment and controller (P2)**

- No SMPL-X body models on the pod (registration-walled; BONES-SEED's SMPL
  side is an unextracted tar on read-only `/data`), so the motion library is
  demo-only: the 76 demos + mirrors + 7 augmentations = 1216 clips. The token
  space therefore covers tabletop handover reaching only. GMR is registered
  for the dual FR3 as a dormant path (`harness/lane_b/gmr/`), one body-model
  download away, but GMR's save path also assumes a floating base.
- Isaac Sim 5.1's MJCF importer is not enabled by the headless Kit experience
  and, enabled by hand, wrote empty USD layers and hung; the Isaac side spawns
  a URDF generated from the same MJCF. The URDF importer turns joint
  `friction` into a PhysX friction coefficient (menagerie's 1.137 N·m glued
  the joints), so URDF dynamics are 0/0. Self-collision is off (convex hulls
  interpenetrate at the folded ready pose; inter-arm contact is not
  modelled).
- Joint-6 convention: SONIC's q6 = FR3 q6 − 2.5307 rad, because the demos
  drive the left joint 6 beyond π and the motion library's axis-angle round
  trip wraps by 2π. Action scale 0.25 rad per unit (SONIC's effort/stiffness
  formula would not reach the demos' excursions); PD gains 400/80 (joints
  1–4) and 200/20 (5–7), the franka repo's high-PD tier.
- SONIC's humanoid-only terms (feet, undesired contacts, foot position, push)
  nulled; anchor = the fixed base; tracked bodies = link2/link4/link7 of each
  arm; the teleop and SMPL encoders stay in the module with zero sample
  probability because the ONNX export enumerates all three. The first RL
  smoke created one online wandb run before the harness switched to offline.
- RL budget: the plain variant 3563 PPO iterations in 1.75 h (2048 envs, one
  GPU) plateaued at 0.68 rad joint error with 5 cm body error; a first
  joint-space-reward variant (`jp`) was stopped at ≈ 900 iterations because
  its kernel had no gradient at the starting error; the second (`jp2`, every
  SONIC kernel at default + a joint-position term of weight 2) trained 2301

**Lane B decoder ceiling fix (P5, 13:00–23:20 UTC)**

- P5 retrained the decoder because P3's `jp2` could not execute its own tokens
  (B-oracle 0/20). Two root causes, both cross-environment mismatches rather
  than tracking failures, found in this order:
- **Plant mismatch (21:05 UTC).** SONIC trained with gravity ON, while lane A's
  handover env spawns both FR3 arms with `disable_gravity=True` (and 400/80 PD
  on all seven joints where SONIC ran 200/20 on joints 5–7, plus different
  armature and physics rate). The decoders had learned to command above the
  reference to cancel sag on the 45°-tilted bases; in the gravity-free handover
  env those same targets put the left hand 5–8 cm too high (+5…+7 cm of it in
  z) and the gripper closed beside the cube — with the plant tracking the
  targets to 0.015 rad, i.e. the decoder was accurate and wrong. Fix: train on
  lane A's plant (`harness/lane_b/robots_dual_fr3_lanea.py`, robot type
  `dual_fr3_lanea`, gravity off, PD 400/80, armature 0, 100 Hz physics with
  decimation 2) — recipe `jp20`.
- **Reset mismatch (22:06 UTC).** SONIC resets ON the reference WITH the
  reference joint velocity; the handover env starts the arm at rest. The first
  ≈ 25 frames of every oracle episode then ran 9–11 cm / 0.2–0.7 rad off the
  demo and the episodes whose left grip closes early missed the cube. Fix on
  the training side, legitimate for the P6 server too (its first tokens also
  arrive with the arm at rest): `jp22` adds reset noise
  `joint_velocity_range [-1.5, 1.5]`, `joint_position_range [-0.1, 0.1]`.
- The `jp` series in between: `jp3`–`jp13` turned the joint-space reward up
  (`tracking_joint_space` weight 4 → 25, a far kernel, a linear −|Δq| penalty
  to −32), which took the SONIC-env replay error 0.775 → 0.055 rad and finally
  made the policy execute the left wrist roll it had been parking in another IK
  branch; `jp14`/`jp15`/`jp18` added a tight hand kernel (std 0.03 m) and
  fingertip-depth reward points, halving the grasp-frame flange error to
  ≈ 1.0 cm. All of them still scored 0/20 in the ceiling test. Warm starts
  (`--checkpoint`: policy + value weights, fresh optimizer) chained the
  variants; a cold start under a joint-error termination (`jp4`) and a warm one
  (`jp5`) both failed to learn and were stopped by their recorded PIDs, as were
  the recon-aux (`jp8`) and exploration (`jp9`) levers once dominated.
- Ceiling-test ranking, 17 tests of 20 rollouts each (STATUS 23:03 / 23:20):
  `jp20` last.pt 19/20 (twice) > `jp22` last.pt 16/20 > `jp22` ckpt 1000 15/20

  across the whole `jp20` series while the ceiling moves from 1/20 to 19/20.
  Only the 20-rollout oracle ranks a decoder.
- Grasp precision, measured rather than assumed (21:05 UTC): the A-oracle

## 7. What breaks at scale — deltas for a real run

1. **Demos.** 76 scripted-source MimicGen episodes give success rates with a
   ≈ 15-point noise floor at n = 20 and one task instance. A real run needs
   ≥ 1000 generated episodes from ≥ 50 human/teleop sources, the MimicGen
   keep-rate captured (flush the tally to a file, not stdout), and a
   validation split held out of both the fine-tune and lane B's motion
   library.
2. **Lane A's labels.** `ik_target_clamped` replays but carries saturated
   first-chunk commands the policy copies. Either record demos with a
   bounded-excursion controller, retrain on next-state labels with a slower
   oracle expectation, or add the inference-side excursion clamp and measure
   it into its own run folder. This is the cheapest experiment left undone.
3. **Motion library.** The full BONES-SEED upper-body subset retargeted
   through GMR (needs the SMPL-X body models, a fixed-base-aware GMR save
   path, the GMR-pkl → SONIC-pkl converter) on top of the demos, ≈ 1k clips
   plus mirrors, so the token space covers more than the demos' own
   trajectories.
4. **SONIC RL.** P5 closed the recipe gaps and they belong in a real run from
   iteration 0: train on the **deployment plant** (gravity flag, PD gains,
   armature, physics rate — the gravity mismatch alone was 5–8 cm at the hand),
   reset with velocity and position noise so the policy has seen an at-rest
   start, keep the joint-space tracking term and the fingertip reward points,
   and rescale the body kernels from humanoid std (0.3 m / 0.4 rad) to arm
   scale. What remains is scale and coverage: 4096 envs × 8 GPUs for ≥ 24 h
   (NVIDIA's recipe) instead of 1.5 h on one GPU warm-started off a chain of
   short runs; the full motion library from item 3 instead of the demos' own
   trajectories; self-collision on (still off here); and re-grasp precision —
   P5's tolerance test puts the right arm's blind grasp of the placed cube at
   ≈ 1 cm safe and 3 cm marginal, against the 1.0–1.3 cm grasp-frame flange
   error the winner reaches, so that margin is thin by construction. Keep the
   gate P5 and P6 now follow: **a 20-rollout B-oracle at ≥ 15/20 before any
   lane B fine-tune**. The teacher-forced replay error is not a substitute for
   it — it spanned only 0.041–0.051 rad over decoders scoring 1/20 to 19/20.
5. **Fine-tune budget.** 20 000 steps for both lanes (NVIDIA's default; the
   prototype ran 10 % of it), same seeds, checkpoints at 5000-step intervals
   or model-only saves (34 GB per full checkpoint means 1.4 TB per lane at
   `--save-steps 500`), and an open-loop eval for lane B as well.
6. **Evaluation.** ≥ 100 rollouts per row (≈ 400 per row to resolve a
   10-point difference near 50 % at 95 % confidence with 80 % power),
   several checkpoints per lane, the same seeds
   for every row, the commanded targets logged per step so jerk and
   excursion can be reported, and the policy server on its own device.
7. **Infrastructure.** All 8 devices were allocatable today once the
   allocator's idle-memory threshold was raised from 1 GiB to 40 GiB
   (`fc7d22b`), and P5 used up to seven concurrent trainers on them; P0–P3 ran
   on 2 of 8 and were sequential because of it. Still missing: a CUDA toolkit,
   working NCCL P2P, storage far above the 600 GB floor (home is 99 % full on a
   shared filesystem — it was at ≈ 2.6 TB free for P5/P6 but every P3 artifact
   still sits on non-persistent instance storage), and an allocator job name
   unique across run roots; the one that is not cost P5 a double-booked device.
8. **Real cell.** Both lanes emit absolute joint targets at 50 Hz. Running
   either on the physical FR3s needs a joint-impedance controller on the
   robots (libfranka joint impedance or ros2_control), which the repo does
   not have today, plus the per-step excursion cap from
   `policy_convention.yaml` (0.60 rad) enforced on the robot side. Lane B additionally needs the decoder's 10-step proprio
   history reproduced exactly on hardware (gravity direction, base angular
   velocity = 0 for the fixed base, joint positions relative to the default
   pose, velocities, last action) — and, as P5 showed the expensive way, a
   plant whose gravity handling and gains match what the decoder was trained
   on. Neither stack is closer to hardware than the other after this prototype.
